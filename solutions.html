Commercial models

Four ways to work with us

The commercial structure is a negotiation, not a form. Most partners start with wholesale and migrate to white label as their volume grows.

Wholesale

You buy data bundles at partner rates and set your own retail price. The widest margin control, and the simplest place to start. Physical SIM cards are priced by volume tier.

Revenue share

We operate the platform, the fulfilment and the support, and we share the margin on what you sell. The lowest operational load on your side.

White label

Your brand, logo and packaging. Our network, provisioning and billing. The customer buys from you and never sees our name.

Embedded / API

We plug into your checkout or app through the partner API. Provisioning, top-ups and usage events run inside your own product.

Model Who buys the data Who sets the retail price What your customer sees Where the platform runs
Wholesale You You Your brand on the voucher or card Our provisioning, your sales process
Revenue share We do Jointly agreed Your brand on a hosted page Our platform, end to end
White label You, at partner rates You Your brand in the handset status bar and on the packaging Our network and billing under your identity

A staged commercial path

Start on wholesale, prove the volume and the support load, then move to white label once the numbers justify artwork, packaging and SPN work. The migration is a commercial amendment, not a re-platforming project.

Integration

The partner API

Four core operations run the whole commercial lifecycle. Everything else is read access and events around them.

provision

Provision a new eSIM and return the activation details your customer needs.

catalogue

Query the available catalogue: destinations, bundle sizes and the rates your account is entitled to.

addPlan

Add a data plan to an eSIM the customer already holds — a top-up, with nothing shipped.

removePlan

Remove a plan, for a cancelled order or a profile that has to be revoked and returned to inventory.

What surrounds them

  • Catalogue browsing — destinations, bundle sizes and your entitled rates
  • Order creation and retrieval, so a purchase maps to your own order reference
  • ICCID lookup to reconcile a profile against your records
  • Usage retrieval — remaining data and balance on an active plan
  • Activation status, so your support team can answer without escalating
  • Real-time webhooks pushed to your endpoint at 50%, 80% and 100% of bundle data

Illustrative pseudo-code

Not a real endpoint. The shape of a call, not a specification.

  • provision — order reference, destination, bundle size → activation details
  • catalogue — destination query → available bundles and entitled rates
  • addPlan — ICCID, bundle size, order reference → confirmation
  • removePlan — ICCID, plan reference → confirmation

Webhook example

  • ICCID, percentage used (50 / 80 / 100), timestamp
Documentation is issued during onboarding

Full reference documentation, sandbox credentials and a Postman collection are released to verified partners as part of onboarding. There is no self-service public developer portal — an engineer is named to your account and walks your team through the four calls, the webhook contract and error handling.

Order flow

New purchase or top-up?

Every transaction is one of two things. The distinction matters commercially, because only one of them involves shipping, and only one of them has a deadline attached.

New purchase

A new eSIM, or a new physical SIM card. A physical card requires shipping to your address or your customer's address, with the lead time and cost that implies.

  • New eSIM — provisioned on demand, nothing shipped
  • New physical SIM — manufactured, printed and shipped
  • White-label artwork and packaging applied before dispatch
  • Unit price set by volume tier

Top-up / recharge

An additional data plan added to a card the customer already holds. Nothing is shipped, so there is no delivery cost and no delivery delay.

  • No shipping, no new inventory, no new ICCID
  • Fulfilled in seconds through the partner API or a hosted page
  • An eSIM can be topped up for up to twelve months after its last usage
  • The natural repeat-revenue event in the relationship
Why the twelve-month window matters

A customer who travels once a year is still inside the top-up window when the next trip is booked. Build the reminder, and a first purchase becomes an annuity rather than a one-off.

Onboarding

From signature to first live transaction

Four steps, typically two to four weeks, with test credit so your developers can integrate before you spend anything.

1

Verification

Company registration, a named responsible contact, and your brand assets if you are white-labelling. Market-by-market compliance is checked here.

2

Account & test credit

We open your partner account, agree the commercial model, set the deposit and load test credit for a pilot order.

3

Integration or white label

Integrate the four core API calls, or let our design team produce your SIM artwork, packaging and message copy.

4

Go live

Pilot campaign, agreed support contact and response time, then scale to production volumes with volume-tier pricing.

Two to four weeks typical

Two weeks is achievable when you integrate the API against your own storefront. Four weeks is typical when artwork, packaging and the Service Provider Name are all being produced for the first time.

Pilot programme

Test us before you commit

We would rather earn the volume than ask you to bet on it. A pilot is deliberately small, tightly defined and commercially risk-free.

No minimum guaranteed volume

During a pilot there is no minimum guaranteed volume. If activation rates or support response times do not meet what we promised, you walk away with nothing owed beyond the orders you placed.

What a pilot defines

  • A defined quantity — small enough to be a rounding error in your budget, large enough to be real
  • Defined destinations — the markets your customers actually fly to, not the whole coverage list
  • A defined period — a fixed window with a review date at the end of it
  • No minimum guaranteed volume — no penalty if the pilot does not convert
  • A success measure agreed up front: activation rate, support tickets per hundred, and repeat top-up rate
