2. The opportunity

Travel connectivity is moving from a roaming charge on a phone bill to a product that travel sellers can retail. Three things changed in the last two years.

2.1 Demand is real and verifiable

  • Global travel eSIM revenue reached USD 1.8 billion in 2025, up 85% in a single year, and is

forecast to reach USD 8.7 billion by 2030 — Juniper Research, October 2025.

  • 1.52 billion international tourist arrivals were recorded in 2025, up 4% on 2024, with

international tourism receipts around USD 1.9 trillion — UN Tourism.

  • Travel eSIMs are roughly 75% cheaper per GB than traditional roaming by 2029. That gap is

the reason a traveller switches, and it is the reason a reseller can make a margin and still be obviously better value than the alternative — Juniper Research.

  • The enterprise eSIM management market is compounding at 31.3% a year, from USD 2.21 billion in 2025 to USD 8.63 billion by 2030 — roughly 2.4 times faster than the headline eSIM market. That platform layer is precisely where a wholesale partnership sits — The Business Research Company.

2.2 The devices have already switched

  • 37% of smartphones sold globally in 2025 were eSIM-capable, forecast to reach **48% in

2026** — Counterpoint Research.

  • 605 million eSIMs shipped in 2025, up 18% year on year, with consumer profile downloads up

43% — Trusted Connectivity Alliance.

  • Apple has removed the physical SIM tray entirely in twelve markets for its latest

generation — the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan, Guam, the US Virgin Islands, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. For inbound travellers from those markets, eSIM is not an option. It is the only choice.

2.3 The channel is still wide open

The B2B2C layer — selling connectivity through travel agencies, OTAs, airlines, insurers and loyalty programmes — remains fragmented. Juniper Research's own competitor leaderboard lists sixteen travel eSIM enablers, and none of them publishes a per-GB rate card. Rates are negotiated privately, which means the partner who gets to market first with a credible, audited supply relationship sets the price in their segment.

The window is open now and it is closing. Every quarter that passes, another OTA or airline adds connectivity to its checkout.

4.2 Two product formats, and the difference matters commercially

eSIM. Nothing is manufactured, nothing is shipped, nothing sits in a warehouse and nothing clears customs. The profile is provisioned on demand and delivered by QR code, one-tap install link, or a printed insert — whichever suits your channel. There is no minimum stock order.

Physical SIM. Still necessary in markets such as Hong Kong where both formats remain in everyday use, and in travel-kit and retail-chain channels where a tangible card is part of the product. Supplied in tiered volumes.

Our recommendation: lead with eSIM. It removes inventory risk, working capital and logistics from your P&L, and it is the direction the device base is already moving.

4.3 New purchase or top-up — the distinction that drives repeat revenue

This is the single most commercially important feature in the platform.

  • New purchase — a new eSIM or SIM card. Physical cards require shipping.
  • Top-up — additional data on a card the customer already holds. Nothing is shipped.

An eSIM can be topped up for up to twelve months after its last usage. A customer who travels again simply buys more data on the profile they already have. That second sale carries no new customer-acquisition cost, which is where the unit economics of this channel become genuinely attractive. Most partners see their margin improve materially in the second year purely from top-up attach.

8. Why partner with us

1. Network ownership, not resale. We own the mobile core network and the billing system, and we interconnect directly with China Mobile, China Unicom, China Mobile Hong Kong, Hutchison 3 Hong Kong, SmarTone Hong Kong, CITIC Telecom and China Telecom Macau. That is materially different from a reseller marking up somebody else's aggregator.

2. A structural advantage on China traffic. In October 2025 China's MIIT approved a nationwide smartphone eSIM pilot, and all three state carriers launched simultaneously. A supplier with a Guangzhou network core and direct interconnect with China Mobile and China Unicom is structurally advantaged on outbound-China traffic exactly as the domestic device base switches on — an advantage a foreign wholesaler cannot legally replicate.

3. Rate transparency. We publish our regional entry rates and we give 30 days' written notice before any cost change. You will never have a live campaign repriced underneath you.

4. Activation reliability treated as a product feature. Activation problems are the single most common support ticket in this industry. We build for them: profile re-issue, revoke-and-return to your inventory, and escalation to a named engineer with an agreed response time — not an upsell.

5. Compliance handled. More than 155 countries now operate mandatory SIM-registration regimes, covering over 60% of global mobile connections. Data-only travel profiles carry no local number and therefore usually fall outside them. In Hong Kong specifically, a corporate customer may register up to 25 pre-paid SIM cards per operator, while there is no cap of that kind on service-plan SIMs — a concrete argument for the eSIM route in corporate channels. We confirm the position country by country when we design your product.

6. Five support languages. English, Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese, Korean and Japanese, with a direct escalation path to engineering.
